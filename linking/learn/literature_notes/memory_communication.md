## 1. Neural cellular automata (NCA) for morphogenesis, self-repair and control: what do they show about generalization to new targets, robustness and training stability?

### Takeaway
NCA reliably buy local-rule robustness and self-repair. Training is fragile, though: it uses BPTT with long rollouts, needs sample pools and damage injection, and shows loss spikes and large run-to-run variance. NCA also generalize poorly to new targets, since the standard recipe is one model per shape, and they suffer a "global communication bottleneck" because information diffuses one cell per step. The most relevant 2026 result, Verma et al.'s NCA-driven lattice swarm assembly, still trains one NCA per morphology. It needed custom fine-tuning (sparse-retention plus a false-positive loss) to cope with physical robots arriving one at a time; with it, F1 rose from 0.58 to 0.99.

### Cited Findings
**Foundational [background]**
- Growing NCA: a shared per-cell update rule (3x3 perception, hidden channels, stochastic per-cell updates) grows a target image from a seed. Persistent behaviour beyond the training horizon needed a **sample pool**, and regeneration needed explicit **damage during training** (random circular masks; the highest-loss sample is reset to the seed). Training showed **sudden loss jumps late in training**, which per-variable L2 gradient normalization reduced. Identical settings could yield models with very different long-term behaviour. Grown patterns can **overgrow** after reaching the target. — [Mordvintsev, Randazzo, Niklasson, Levin, "Growing Neural Cellular Automata," Distill 2020](https://distill.pub/2020/growing-ca/) (summary details via search snippet)
- 3D NCA grew Minecraft structures, including functional machines, and regenerated them. — [Sudhakaran, Grbic, Li, Katona, Najarro, Glanois, Risi, "Growing 3D Artefacts and Functional Machines with Neural Cellular Automata," ALIFE 2021, arXiv:2103.08737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08737) [background][not re-fetched]
- NCAs are hard to steer once growth starts. Goal-Guided NCA injects a goal encoding at every step so cell behaviour can be controlled dynamically. — [Sudhakaran, Najarro, Risi, "Goal-Guided Neural Cellular Automata: Learning to Control Self-Organising Systems," arXiv:2205.06806 (2022)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.06806) [background]
- NCAs regenerate damaged soft-robot morphologies in simulation. — [Horibe, Walker, Risi, "Regenerating Soft Robots through Neural Cellular Automata," EuroGP 2021, arXiv:2102.02579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02579) [background][not re-fetched]
- A single NCA both grows a modular robot from a seed and then acts as its controller, routing control signals through the same cells. — [NCRS, "A Single Neural Cellular Automaton for Body-Brain Co-evolution," GECCO 2022 Companion, DOI 10.1145/3520304.3529002](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3520304.3529002) [background] (snippet only)
- Graph NCA generalize the lattice to arbitrary graphs. This is the bridge between NCA and GNN message passing. — [Grattarola, Livi, Alippi, "Learning Graph Cellular Automata," NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2110.14237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14237) [background][not re-fetched]

**Swarm and lattice assembly with NCA (2026, most relevant to this project)**
- Verma, Ferrante and Tzes pair NCA with site assignment and collision-aware control on a **2D square lattice** with 4-neighbour attachment sites. Attached robots ("Advertisers") each keep a persistent 5x5 patch of NCA states, refreshed from broadcasts by attached neighbours. They apply the shared NCA update once per control step and advertise empty cardinal neighbours whose alpha exceeds a threshold. Arriving robots ("Joiners") average alpha across advertisers and initialise their own state from an advertised state vector. — [Verma, Ferrante, Tzes, "Distributed Swarm Morphogenesis with Neural Cellular Automata," arXiv:2610.31900 (Oct 2026, cs.RO)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31900v2)
- The NCA was trained offline as a standard Growing NCA: 16 channels, 3x3 neighbourhood, stochastic updates, BPTT over 64–96 steps. It was then fine-tuned with **sparse-retention rollouts**, which randomly retain candidate sites as newly attached and zero state outside occupied cells and their neighbours, to simulate gradual physical assembly. A **false-positive loss** penalises alpha above 0.2 on background cells. On the Tree shape with 250 robots over 30 seeds, F1 was **0.583 ± 0.107** for the standard NCA, **0.764 ± 0.141** with sparse-retention fine-tuning, and **0.988 ± 0.004** with the false-positive loss added. — [Verma et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31900v2)
- Limitations of that work: **one NCA per morphology**, no held-out shapes, a fixed seed, and orientation set by the first joiner. Narrow shapes (Lizard, Hand-heart) form more slowly. Hardware used 8 Crazyflies with external Lighthouse tracking and centralised computation of local-frame observations. Scaling reached "several hundred robots" in simulation. — [Verma et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31900v2)

**2024–2026 NCA findings on generalization, robustness and scale**
- A 2026 survey of NCA applications says growing NCAs "must be retrained for each image," and most NCAs are trained for a single class or texture. Partial fixes include reprogramming via one symmetric matrix (Randazzo 2021) and encoding multiple textures in the seed (Catrina 2024). — [Lemke, Ihm, Kalkhof, … Mukhopadhyay, "Applications of Neural Cellular Automata: State of the Art, Challenges and Opportunities," arXiv:2609.24595 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24595v1)
- The same survey names a **"global communication bottleneck"**: information diffuses slowly through many iterations. Multi-scale and hybrid designs help (Kalkhof 2024, Lemke 2025, Yang 2025, Pio-Lopez 2026), but no general solution exists. Training by BPTT is memory-heavy and slow. — [Lemke et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24595v1)
- On robustness, an NCA beat a UNet on out-of-distribution retinal OCT and cardiac MRI data (Korevaar et al. 2024), although it was slightly worse in-distribution on MRI. The survey lists **no NCA-based robot control or policy-learning papers**. Swarm-like work appears only as self-classifying hardware tiles (Walker 2022, the only physical deployment), 3D self-recognising bricks (Moreno 2026, simulation) and self-healing logic circuits (Béna 2026). — [Lemke et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24595v1)
- NCA applied to ARC-AGI: asynchronous updates during training reduced overfitting and improved robustness, but the approach "showed limits in propagating global context." — [NCA for ARC-AGI, arXiv:2506.15746 (June 2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15746) (snippet)
- "Reasoning with NCA" reports OOD generalization to harder tasks when test-time compute grows (more space, time or rollouts). This depends on training with sample replay and stochastic perturbations, and the models repair damage well. — [arXiv:2609.36126 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36126v1) (snippet)
- Compact NCAs (about 9.8k parameters) outperform recurrent and feed-forward baselines on few-shot and scale-variant MNIST. The hidden channels appear to encode scale-invariant structure rather than templates. — [“Neural Cellular Automata Learn General Features in their Hidden Channels,” arXiv:2609.21870 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.21870) (snippet)
- Neural Particle Automata replace the grid with particles that carry positions and states, sharing one rule and using differentiable SPH operators for neighbourhood perception. Robustness and regeneration carry over from NCA, and the model reaches 98.42% on self-classifying point clouds. — [Kim et al., "Neural Particle Automata: Learning Self-Organizing Particle Dynamics," arXiv:2601.16096 (Jan 2026), KAIST/EPFL](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16096v2) (snippet)
- Petri Dish NCA argue that standard NCA freeze their update rule during development, and instead train continually during an open-ended simulation. — [Sakana AI, "Petri Dish Neural Cellular Automata" (2025)](https://pub.sakana.ai/pdnca/) (snippet)
- An information-dynamics study attributes NCA self-maintenance to high-dimensional nonlinear collective dynamics in which internal fluctuations play a role. — ["Structured Fluctuations and the Information Dynamics of Self-Maintenance in Growing NCA," Entropy 28(8):893 / arXiv:2607.12403 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.12403) (snippet)

### Inferences
- An NCA is, structurally, what the project plans: a shared per-agent rule, a recurrent hidden state and local message exchange. The NCA literature's training recipes transfer directly: sample pools for persistence, damage or perturbation injection, stochastic (asynchronous) updates, and gradient normalisation.
- Verma et al. show that an NCA trained on "instant whole-grid" growth fails when physical agents arrive incrementally (F1 0.58). The training rollouts must reproduce the deployment process, here partial occupancy. For this project, the learned message-passing module should be trained inside the actual slide/pivot simulator, or at least in rollouts that mimic incremental occupancy. Training on an idealized grid alone is not enough.
- The NCA field has not solved "one model, many targets." The project's existing MLP already builds an untrained shape because it conditions on a target-shape observation. That conditioning should be kept: the target should be an input, not baked into the weights, as Goal-Guided NCA does and Verma's per-shape NCA does not.
- The global-communication bottleneck matters for decisions like "seal off a cavity." Several message rounds per tick, multi-scale aggregation, or keeping the hand-designed hop-count gradient as a long-range channel are all plausible mitigations.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Variational NCA (Palm et al., ICLR 2022) and Diffusion NCA (Kalkhof et al. 2024) results on multi-target generalization, and the 2026 *Neural Networks* paper on scalability in active NCAs (title only seen).
- No 3D-lattice, connectivity-constrained, NCA-controlled robot assembly paper was found. Verma et al. is 2D and drone-based, with no connectivity-preserving motion constraints.
- No paper found compares NCA-style learned messages against a hand-designed gradient in the same assembly task.

## 2. GNN policies for modular robots and swarms: consensus on scaling to more agents than trained on

### Takeaway
There is strong and repeated evidence that GNN or permutation-invariant message-passing policies zero-shot transfer to larger teams. Reported examples: 3 to over 100 robots, 4 to 100, 10 to 50, and 100 to 500. The gains depend on graph density and degrade when train and test team sizes differ greatly. Equivariance (local canonicalization), one-hop locality, and curriculum or fine-tuning from smaller teams are the 2024–2026 levers. Real-robot GNN deployments remain small, at 5–8 robots, and often rely on external tracking.

### Cited Findings
**Background (pre-2024)**
- Dynamic Graph Networks: modular agents (limbs) that self-assemble, with a shared modular policy passing messages along the current morphology graph, generalized to unseen morphologies and numbers of limbs better than monolithic policies. — [Pathak, Lu, Darrell, Isola, Efros, "Learning to Control Self-Assembling Morphologies: A Study of Generalization via Modularity," NeurIPS 2019, arXiv:1902.05546](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.05546) [background][not re-fetched]
- Shared Modular Policies: one policy whose modules pass messages up and down the kinematic tree controls many morphologies and zero-shot transfers to unseen ones. — [Huang, Mordatch, Pathak, "One Policy to Control Them All," ICML 2020, arXiv:2007.04976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.04976) [background][not re-fetched]
- GNN controllers imitating a centralized flocking controller worked with local communication and transferred to larger swarms. — [Tolstaya, Gama, Paulos, Pappas, Kumar, Ribeiro, "Learning Decentralized Controllers for Robot Swarms with Graph Neural Networks," CoRL 2019, arXiv:1903.10527](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.10527) [background][not re-fetched]
- A GNN path-planning policy trained on small teams generalized to about 6x the instance size seen in training. — [Li, Gama, Ribeiro, Prorok, "Graph Neural Networks for Decentralized Multi-Robot Path Planning," IROS 2020, arXiv:1912.06095](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.06095) [background] (snippet)
- Graph Policy Gradients learn K-hop local filters and transfer policies trained on 3 robots to over 100 without retraining. — [Khan, Tolstaya, Ribeiro, Kumar, "Graph Policy Gradients for Large Scale Robot Control," CoRL 2019, arXiv:1907.03822](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.03822v4) [background]
- A spatial GNN for coverage was trained with 4 agents and tested on up to 100. — [Tolstaya, Paulos, Kumar, Ribeiro, "Multi-Robot Coverage and Exploration using Spatial Graph Neural Networks," arXiv:2011.01119](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.01119) [background] (snippet)
- First real-world fully decentralized GNN deployment: ROS2 framework, ad hoc inter-robot communication, 5 robots through a narrow passage. Later work notes it relied on global positioning and a simple state space. — [Blumenkamp, Morad, Gielis, Li, Prorok, "A Framework for Real-World Multi-Robot Systems Running Decentralized GNN-Based Policies," ICRA 2022, arXiv:2111.01777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.01777) [background] (snippet). No 2024 Blumenkamp follow-up was found in this session.

**2024–2026**
- A GNN policy for decentralized unlabeled motion planning was trained on 100 robots and tested on up to 500, outperforming state-of-the-art methods by 8.6% on average. The authors attribute the smaller gains in small teams to denser communication graphs. — [“Generalizability of Graph Neural Networks for Decentralized Unlabeled Motion Planning,” arXiv:2409.19829 (Sep 2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.19829) (snippet)
- A follow-up graph-planning paper cautions that "the more robots we add, the more the GNNs degrade" when training used few robots. — [“Graph Neural Planning and Predictive Control for Multi-Robot Communication-Constrained Unlabeled Motion Planning,” arXiv:2605.19209 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.19209) (snippet)
- DGNN-GA goal assignment was trained on 10 robots and zero-shot generalized to 50, with an explicit trade-off between training team size and zero-shot performance. — [“Graph Neural Network for Decentralized Multi-Robot Goal Assignment” (NSF PAR)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10557788) (snippet; year unconfirmed)
- LEGO combines agent-centric local canonicalization (making the policy E(2)-equivariant) with role-aware graph encoders. With MAPPO it beat MLP, graph-only, canonicalization-only and equivariant baselines on MPE Spread and Tag, transferred without fine-tuning to unseen team sizes, benefited from curriculum initialization from smaller teams, and ran on Crazyflies, staying operational after one pursuer was disabled. — [Wang, Zhong, Chang, Allen-Blanchette, "Local-Canonicalization Equivariant GNNs for Sample-Efficient and Generalizable Swarm Robot Control," arXiv:2509.14431, IROS 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14431)
- A swarm sim-to-real RL study reports that policies with **one-hop** communication scaled better than globally communicating ones and cut bandwidth from O(n²) to O(1). — [“Structural bias enables reliable sim-to-real transfer in reinforcement learning for robot swarms” (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/416518567_Structural_bias_enables_reliable_sim-to-real_transfer_in_reinforcement_learning_for_robot_swarms) (snippet; authors and venue not confirmed)
- A morphology-aware graph RL policy for tensegrity locomotion (2025) shows the "graph over body" idea in active use. — [arXiv:2510.26067](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26067) (title or snippet only)

### Inferences
- The project's cube robots on a lattice have a natural, dynamic neighbour graph of at most 6 face neighbours, which is exactly the GNN or graph-NCA setting. The size-generalization results support sharing weights across agents, using permutation-invariant aggregation over neighbours, and training at modest swarm sizes. These results come from navigation and coverage tasks, not construction.
- Local canonicalization (expressing observations in the bot's own frame) is the GNN analogue of the "map compass" the project added. The LEGO results suggest pairing frame-relative features with an explicit global-orientation cue only where needed.
- Expect degradation when graph density or diameter at test time differs strongly from training, as noted in arXiv:2409.19829 and 2605.19209. Varying swarm size and shape during training, or a size curriculum, is the evidence-backed mitigation.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 GNN paper on lattice self-reconfiguration or assembly with connectivity constraints was found.
- Full quantitative tables for LEGO and DGNN-GA were not retrieved.

## 3. Learned communication in MARL (CommNet, DIAL/RIAL, TarMAC, IC3Net) and 2024+ critiques

### Takeaway
Learned communication clearly helps on tasks constructed to require it, such as traffic junctions and hidden-goal tasks. Elsewhere, the critiques are consistent. Gains over a well-tuned no-communication baseline are often small. Ablating messages can barely change performance, which is a sign the messages are ignored. Purely emergent protocols from a sparse shared reward are fragile, hyperparameter-sensitive and fail to scale with problem size. Benchmarks, metrics and bandwidth accounting are not standardized. Structured or engineered message content, such as predicted intentions or plans, tends to outperform unstructured emergent bits.

### Cited Findings
**Background**
- DIAL/RIAL: differentiable inter-agent learning, which backpropagates through the message channel during centralized training, beat no-communication baselines on purpose-built communication tasks. — [Foerster, Assael, de Freitas, Whiteson, "Learning to Communicate with Deep Multi-Agent RL," NeurIPS 2016](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/file/c7635bfd99248a2cdef8249ef7bfbef4-Paper.pdf) [background]
- CommNet: continuous averaged broadcast messages, learned end-to-end. — [Sukhbaatar, Szlam, Fergus, NeurIPS 2016, arXiv:1605.07736](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.07736) [background][not re-fetched]
- TarMAC: targeted, attention-based multi-round messages. — [Das et al., ICML 2019, arXiv:1810.11187](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11187) [background][not re-fetched]
- IC3Net: learned gating of when to communicate. — [Singh, Jain, Sukhbaatar, ICLR 2019, arXiv:1812.09755](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.09755) [background][not re-fetched]
- Positive signalling and listening metrics are needed. Reward gains alone do not prove that agents use messages. — [Lowe, Foerster, Boureau, Pineau, Dauphin, "On the Pitfalls of Measuring Emergent Communication," AAMAS 2019, arXiv:1903.05168](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.05168) [background][not re-fetched]
- On the traffic junction task, the no-communication baseline reached about 79% success in medium mode versus over 97% for TarMAC-based methods, and about 48% in hard mode, where only I2C+TarMAC exceeded 90%. — [Ding, Huang, Lu, "Learning Individually Inferred Communication for Multi-Agent Cooperation (I2C)," NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/fb2fcd534b0ff3bbed73cc51df620323-Paper.pdf) [background] (snippet)
- In networked traffic control, fixed communication beat no communication but was worse than emergent communication. — [“Networked Multi-Agent RL with Emergent Communication,” arXiv:2004.02780 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.02780) [background] (snippet)
- In one 2023 study, a learned communication method (TarMAC-QMix comparison) was more harmful than no communication at all. — [Hu et al., "Learning Practical Communication Strategies in Cooperative MARL," ACML 2023 (PMLR v189)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v189/hu23a/hu23a.pdf) [background] (snippet; truncated, verify)

**2024–2026 critiques and evidence**
- A two-agent partially observable gridworld (NeurIPS 2025 workshop) tested learned 1-bit messages (LDC) by zeroing them out after training. Success barely moved: 89.4% to 88.6% fully observable, and 31.89% to 30.26% partially observable. As the grid grew from 10x10 to 15x15, LDC fell from 30.8% to 12.2%. Messages produced by a world-model "intention" module held at 99.9% and 96.5%, while the no-communication baseline scored 0%. LDC only learned with tiny message alphabets. Larger vocabularies, intrinsic message rewards and absolute coordinates failed or were fragile. The authors conclude that a protocol emerging from "a single, unstructured reward signal is insufficient" and that emergent communication is "powerful but fragile." Caveats: no seeds or confidence intervals are reported, and bandwidth differs between methods. — [Hill, Wei, Jishnuanandh, "Communicating Plans, Not Percepts: Scalable Multi-Agent Coordination with Embodied World Models," arXiv:2508.02912, NeurIPS 2025 SEA workshop](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02912v2)
- A 2026 survey finds communication methods are judged mostly by task reward or convergence speed. It reports a lack of standardized comparison on overhead, bandwidth and scaling, and calls for system-level benchmarking. — [“Communication Methods in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning,” arXiv:2601.12886 (2026)](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12886) (snippet)
- An earlier survey notes that results across communication papers are hard to compare because reward configurations vary even on "the same" benchmark. — [Zhu, Dastani, Wang, "A Survey of Multi-Agent Deep RL with Communication," AAMAS journal 2024 / arXiv:2203.08975](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-023-09633-6) (snippet)
- 2024 methods (DCMAC, context-aware communication) continue to report gains, largely on communication-dependent or self-modified benchmarks. — [DCMAC, arXiv:2409.07127](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.07127); [Context-aware Communication for MARL, arXiv:2312.15600](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15600); [Multi-task multi-agent communication, arXiv:2511.03348](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03348v1) (snippets)
- A secondary summary attributes a 20–40% return drop from disabling messages to Gupta et al. (2020), and notes that ablation reward drops are "necessary but not sufficient" evidence of communication. — [Emergent Mind topic page](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/emergent-multi-agent-communication) (aggregator; verify against the primary source)

### Inferences
- For the cube swarm, a learned message channel will likely be **ignored** unless (a) the task cannot be solved from the 262-number local observation plus the hop count, and (b) training gives gradient through the channel. DIAL-style backprop through messages within a tick is the GNN/NCA default, and it is much easier to optimize than RL-only emergent discrete symbols.
- Run three tests: retrain with messages zeroed (a no-comm baseline), ablate messages at test time, and check whether messages predict non-local facts such as distance to an unsupported overhang or a cavity about to be enclosed. Without them, apparent gains could just come from extra parameters or recurrence.
- The "engineered over emergent" result supports a hybrid design: keep the hand-designed hop-count gradient and add a learned residual channel, or give messages auxiliary prediction targets (for example, predicting a global quantity computed by the simulator) rather than relying on task reward alone.

### Gaps
- No rigorous 2024–2026 cross-benchmark ablation (SMAC, MPE, VMAS) of learned versus no communication under equal tuning was found.
- No work on learned communication specifically for collective construction was found.

## 4. Memory in partially observable RL: recurrent vs transformer vs SSM, and whether memory helps local swarm agents

### Takeaway
In RL (as opposed to supervised sequence modelling), well-tuned GRUs remain the strongest general-purpose memory baseline. Transformers mainly help with long memory length, not credit assignment. SSMs and linear-recurrent models (S4/S5, Mamba-2, MinGRU, memoroids) are competitive and much faster, but sometimes unstable. On many tasks a memoryless MLP is nearly as good, so memory helps only when the task needs it. No 2024–2026 study was found that directly measures memory benefit for local swarm or lattice-assembly agents.

### Cited Findings
- POPGym (15 environment families with difficulty variants, 13 memory models) found a "notable disconnect" between memory-model rankings in supervised learning and RL. **GRU was the best general-purpose memory model**, and Elman RNNs gave the best performance/efficiency trade-off. Newer models (linear transformers, S4D, FWP, FART) did worse. A third-party review reports GRU at 0.349 mean max-mean episodic reward versus 0.112 for FWP and 0.138 for FART, with S4D at -0.180 due to unstable weights. — [Morad, Kortvelesy, Bettini, Liwicki, Prorok, "POPGym: Benchmarking Partially Observable RL," ICLR 2023, arXiv:2303.01859](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01859) [background]; review numbers via [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/popgym-benchmarking-partially-observable-reinforcement-learning) (secondary)
- An ICLR 2024 paper confirms PPO+GRU as the best model-free POPGym baseline, with PPO+LSTM second. — [Samsami et al., "Mastering Memory Tasks with World Models (R2I)," ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/0a0e436e8830f746300f592377730fca-Paper-Conference.pdf) (snippet)
- Memoroids recast linear-recurrent and SSM-style memories as monoids for efficient parallel scans. The paper also proposes tape-based batching instead of segment-based batching for recurrent RL, evaluated with four memoroids on nine POPGym environments over ten seeds. — ["Recurrent Reinforcement Learning with Memoroids," NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/19f7f755908372efb25826d61959cdf9-Paper-Conference.pdf) (snippet; author list believed to be Morad et al., unconfirmed this session)
- POPGym Arcade (2025): on its pixel POMDP suite, a plain MLP totalled 0.45 ± 0.01 versus 0.42 ± 0.02 for MinGRU, though MinGRU did better on the POMDP versions specifically. — ["POPGym Arcade: Parallel Pixelated POMDPs," arXiv:2503.01450](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2503.01450) (snippet)
- Transformers extend memory length in RL but do not improve long-term credit assignment. — [Ni, Ma, Eysenbach, Bacon, "When Do Transformers Shine in RL? Decoupling Memory from Credit Assignment," NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2307.03864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03864) [background][not re-fetched]
- MIKASA-Robo (2025) is a memory-intensive robotic manipulation benchmark. GTrXL and a Mamba baseline both trailed RATE, a transformer with external memory, especially on high-memory RememberColor tasks. — [Cherepanov et al., "Memory, Benchmark & Robots: A Benchmark for Solving Complex Tasks with RL," arXiv:2502.10550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.10550) (snippet); RATE: [arXiv:2306.09459](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.09459)
- RLBenchNet (2025) reviews MemoryGym evidence that well-tuned GRUs can beat Transformer-XL on indefinite-horizon tasks, and claims Mamba-2 is competitive on highly memory-dependent tasks. — [“RLBenchNet: The Right Network for the Right RL Task,” arXiv:2505.15040](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15040) (snippet)
- Sequential models are rarely tested in RL, and there is no unified testbed. — [“State-Space Modeling in Long Sequence Processing: A Survey on Recurrence in the Transformer Era,” arXiv:2406.09062 (2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.09062) (snippet)
- In NCA-style systems, the per-cell hidden channels are the memory. ARC-NCA reports that adding per-cell "private memory tapes" gave stable, capable behaviour across tasks. — [Guichard et al., ARC-NCA, arXiv:2505.08778 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08778) (cited via snippet of arXiv:2506.15746)

### Inferences
- For per-bot memory in the swarm, a small GRU (or minGRU or another linear recurrence, for fast parallel training) is the evidence-backed default. Transformers over per-bot history are unlikely to be worth it, and the planned architecture is already NCA-like, where the hidden state is the memory.
- The MLP-nearly-as-good results warn that recurrence may add little unless the observation is truly insufficient. In this project, plausible memory-dependent quantities include "which pillar am I supporting," "was this region already decided as a cavity," and commitments in build order. Test with ablations: memoryless vs GRU, with and without messages, on held-out shapes.
- Combining memory with message passing turns the swarm into a spatially distributed RNN: messages integrated over ticks act like extra NCA iterations. This partly offsets the per-tick global-communication bottleneck, but it makes training BPTT over time and space, with NCA-style stability issues (sample pools, gradient normalization).

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 study isolates memory benefit for decentralized swarm or modular-robot agents. Evidence comes from single-agent POMDP benchmarks.
- The headline numbers of the memoroids paper were not retrieved.

## 5. Kilobot-like hand-designed gradient / message-passing algorithms vs learned messages

### Takeaway
No published head-to-head comparison was found between a hand-designed hop-count or gradient algorithm and a learned message channel on the same assembly task. The closest evidence is indirect. Hand-designed gradient protocols (Kilobot, TERMES) give provable guarantees for shape formation and construction order. Learned GNN filters over K-hop neighbourhoods replicate and scale centralized or expert behaviour. Imitation-learned policies can outperform their classical expert under communication delay, and optimization or PD controllers still beat learned policies on final precision in some swarm tasks.

### Cited Findings
- Kilobot self-assembly (1,024 robots) used hand-designed gradient formation (hop counts from seed robots), localization and edge-following to build user-specified 2D shapes. — [Rubenstein, Cornejo, Nagpal, "Programmable self-assembly in a thousand-robot swarm," Science 345:795 (2014)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1254295) [background][not re-fetched]
- TERMES: hand-designed local rules (structpath / traffic rules compiled from the target) guarantee that termite-inspired climbing robots build the target structure without creating unreachable sites. This is the classical answer to build-order and "do not seal off" problems. — [Werfel, Petersen, Nagpal, "Designing Collective Behavior in a Termite-Inspired Robot Construction Team," Science 343:754 (2014)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1245842) [background][not re-fetched]
- Hop-count flooding: each node records its hop count from a source on first receipt and rebroadcasts. Averaging over repeated floods gives a distance estimate. — [US Patent 7,433,266 (sensor-network localization)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7433266) (non-peer-reviewed)
- Learned K-hop graph filters transfer from 3 to over 100 robots. — [Khan et al., Graph Policy Gradients, CoRL 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.03822v4) [background]
- In swarm deep RL, a PD/optimization baseline eventually drove mean distance error to zero while learned policies kept a small residual. The learned policies reduced error faster and earned higher average reward. — [Hüttenrauch, Šošić, Neumann, "Deep Reinforcement Learning for Swarm Systems," JMLR 20 (2019)](http://jmlr.org/papers/volume20/18-476/18-476.pdf) [background] (snippet)
- A behaviour-cloned policy trained on a classical artificial-potential-field (ARD-PF) expert under delay outperformed that classical controller in stability and delay tolerance. — [“Delay-Aware UAV Swarm Formation Control via Imitation Learning from ARD-PF Expert Policies” (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/399506992_Delay-Aware_UAV_Swarm_Formation_Control_via_Imitation_Learning_from_ARD-PF_Expert_Policies) (snippet; venue and year unconfirmed)
- Verma et al. (2026) effectively replace a hand-specified attachment-site list with learned NCA state propagation among attached robots, while keeping classical modules for site assignment and collision avoidance. That is a hybrid, not a head-to-head comparison. — [arXiv:2610.31900](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31900v2)
- Engineered, structured messages (predicted intentions) beat emergent 1-bit messages by large margins at scale. — [Hill et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.02912](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02912v2)

### Inferences
- The project's relayed hop count to the work is essentially a Kilobot gradient. The evidence favours keeping it as an input feature and adding learned messages as an extra channel, the hybrid pattern seen in Verma 2026 and Hill 2025, rather than replacing it.
- To show learned messages add value, the fair comparison is: hop-count only, hop-count plus a richer hand-designed message (for example, a TERMES-style reachability or support count), and hop-count plus learned messages. Each needs equal tuning and held-out shapes. No published study provides this comparison for lattice assembly, so it would be a novel result.
- For safety-critical non-local constraints (connectivity, not enclosing cavities), the literature's only guarantees come from hand-designed rules such as TERMES. A learned policy may still need a hard local check, which the project already has as a local fall check.

### Gaps
- No direct empirical comparison was found of hand-designed gradient or message protocols against learned message passing in self-assembly or construction.
- No 2024–2026 work was found on learned replacements for TERMES-style construction-order guarantees.
