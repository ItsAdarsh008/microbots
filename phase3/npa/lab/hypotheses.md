# Hypotheses from failures

Each row starts from something that went wrong or looked bad, states what we think caused it, and names the
experiments that would show whether we are right. Results are added as they arrive. A hypothesis that is
refuted is as useful as one that is confirmed; do not delete them.

| ID | What went wrong (evidence) | Hypothesis | Experiments (config names) | We expect | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H1 | Cutting off the tail tip is never repaired; the tail ends in a blunt stub (`results/repair_cut_tail.png`, paper weights, 4,096 particles). | The paper's model was only trained on state wipes, so nothing taught it to regrow missing mass. Training on deletions will fix cuts. | `train_cut`, `train_cut_drag`, `train_all_damage` | cut excess falls a lot; wipe unchanged; maybe more drift | open |
| H2 | Even if H1 holds, a regrown tip needs to know how far to extend; a body-only memory (16 states) may not hold that. | Private memory channels (not sensed by neighbours) give particles room to store "where am I along the body", improving regrowth of larger cuts. | `gated3_rms_mem8`, `train_all_damage_mem8` | memory helps big cuts more than small ones | open |
| H3 | A torn-off foot stays stranded forever (`results/repair_drag_limb.png`): with no neighbours inside eps a particle senses nothing. | With strict locality at eps this is unfixable by training alone: a particle with no input cannot learn where to go. Training on far drags will not help (control experiment). | `train_far_drag_mem8` | no improvement on drag at 0.8 (4 eps) | open |
| H4 | Same failure as H3. | Sensing a second, wider ring (2 eps) is still local and bounded, and lets fragments within 2 eps rejoin. | `scales2`, `scales2_all_damage` | drag at 0.4 (2 eps) improves; 0.8 does not | open |
| H5 | Stray particles drift off the tail tip and head (`results/baseline_N4096_grow.png`). | Isolated particles get a constant (bias-driven) move. Jostling positions during training teaches particles to rejoin and to be robust to being alone. | `noise_both`, `train_all_damage_noise` | fewer strays; noise damage excess falls | open |
| H6 | The finished lizard keeps changing: undamaged IoU rose from 0.867 to 0.891 over the next 240 steps (4,096 particles). | The trained rollouts (32-96 steps) never required a fixed point. Training on longer rollouts yields a true attractor, which should repair better and drift less. | `long_rollout` | lower `drift`, better excess | open |
| H7 | The paper's 4,096-particle model run on 1,024 particles gives a coarse lizard with merged limbs (`results/repair_N1024_*.png`). | That was a density mismatch (30 neighbours instead of 120), not a limit of the method. A model trained at 1,024 particles with eps 0.2 will be as good as the 4,096 one. | `base` vs the paper weights | similar score | open |
| H8 | Bigger networks fit the target better. | They improve growth but not repair: excess stays the same. | `width256`, `depth3`, `residual3`, `gated3`, `gated3_rms` | score improves, excess does not | open |
| H9 | (Later) A conclusion that holds on the lizard may not hold on shapes with holes, thin parts or branches. | Results transfer across topology, except that thin features (ring, key, anchor) fail whenever they are narrower than eps. | the shape sweep (`shape_*`) | thin shapes fail for every architecture | open |
| H10 | `gated3` (gated residual blocks, zero-init head, no normalisation) diverged to NaN at iteration 221 (`runs/gated3/train.log`). | Unbounded residual growth through 60+ recurrent steps destabilises it; RMSNorm in front of each block bounds it. The trainer now also recovers from blow-ups (back to the last good weights, half the learning rate). | `gated3` (diverged), `gated3_rms`, `gated3_guard` | `gated3_rms` trains; `gated3_guard` survives and ends worse than or equal to `gated3_rms` | open |
| H11 | Every cut leaves a permanent deficit: with N conserved the survivors can only spread thin; the tail tip is never refilled even when trained on cuts (to be confirmed by H1). | The conserved particle count is the root cause of cut failure (the paper lists "no merge or split" as a limitation). Letting particles gain mass, or divide into spare slots, lets a cut be refilled. | phase 2c (see `phase2.md`) | cut excess falls to about the wipe level only with growth | open |
| H12 | The paper and everything so far bake the target into the weights, so one network can heal one shape. | If the shape is carried by the swarm (positions and colours as data) and the network only learns "keep the swarm as it was", it heals unseen shapes of any topology. | phase 2b (see `phase2.md`) | held-out shapes heal nearly as well as training shapes; failures concentrate on thin features and large cuts | open |
| H13 | `gated3_rms_mem8` never learned to grow (control loss 0.98, an untrained swarm): its loss tracked `gated3_rms` to iteration ~400, then exploded to ~70 with density normal, i.e. the state channels ran away (the 100x overflow penalty dominates). The NaN guard did not fire because nothing was NaN. | The private-memory loop (gate and candidate feeding back through the cell) destabilises training at lr 5e-4. A lower learning rate or a global gradient clip keeps it stable. The trainer now also treats a loss spike (6x its recent median) as a blow-up and recovers. | `mem8_lr3e4`, `mem8_clip1`, and `gated3_rms_mem8` rerun with the spike guard (`gated3_rms_mem8_guard`) | at least one of them trains; memory then helps big cuts (H2) or does nothing | open |
| H14 | Real bots have no shared compass (IMU only, no yaw reference): bearings exist only in each bot's own frame (`locomotion_findings.md`). The paper's rule and all our cells read vectors in one global frame. | With a random fixed heading per bot, the plain MLP cell degrades sharply, while a rotation-equivariant cell (invariant features in, equivariant move out) is unaffected. | `heading_*` runs: `mlp` and `equiv` cells with `heading: random`, ideal and physical | MLP: large loss of score; equiv: none | open |
| H15 | The real gait makes 18 to 50 mm strides with 4 s ticks and +-20% length, +-5 deg heading noise; our dots move any distance. | A rule trained on smooth moves loses repair quality under strides with a minimum step and noise; training inside the embodiment recovers most of it. | `deploy.py` twins (`*__phys`), `phys_*` training runs | deploy drops hard excess; in-loop training recovers it | open |
| H16 | IR line of sight leaves 4 to 6 visible neighbours, not 13. | The method still works at eps 0.05 (about 7 neighbours) and 0.04 (about 5), but with worse growth and repair; larger memory helps because there is less to sense. | `eps7_*`, `eps5_*` | graceful degradation, not collapse | open |
| H17 | Models trained on deletions repair cuts but have positive drift (+0.06 to +0.08): the finished lizard slowly degrades by step 1000, and they grow slower (`train_cut`, `train_cut_drag`, `train_all_damage`, 3,000 iterations). | Training on short rollouts with damage never required a stable fixed point; longer rollouts, truncated-window training, or more iterations remove the drift. | `all_damage_long`, `gated_trunc_all_damage`, `all_damage_10k`, `gated_all_damage_10k` | drift back to <= 0 with the cut repair kept | open |
| H18 | Every physical-regime run (`eps13_*`) died in 2 to 3 minutes: initial loss 88 (density 43.8, against 0.9 at eps 0.2), then repeated blow-ups (`runs/eps13_all_damage/train.log`). | This is an artifact, not a limit of small eps: the seed disc was 2 eps wide, so at eps 0.066 the 1,024 particles started about 20x too dense. A seed radius of max(2 eps, 0.4) (the same as before for eps >= 0.2) removes it. | `eps13_*_v2`, `eps7_*`, `eps5_*` | they train; then H16 is a real question | open |
